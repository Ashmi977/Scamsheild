ScamShield is installed

  1. Pin it: open the puzzle-piece menu in the toolbar and pin ScamShield. Alt+Shift+S also opens it.
  2. Check any message: paste it into the popup, select text on a page and choose Check with ScamShield from the right-click menu, or paste a screenshot or QR code.
  3. Optional: turn on inline warnings below to see badges directly on WhatsApp Web and Gmail.

All checks run on this device. ScamShield never sends your messages anywhere.

Inline warnings

Shows a badge next to risky messages while you read them. Turning a site on asks Chrome for permission to read that site only. Messages are checked inside the extension and never leave your browser.

WhatsApp Web

Incoming chat messages. Senders shown as a phone number are treated as not in your contacts.

Gmail

Opened emails, including the real destinations of links hidden behind link text.

Desktop dashboard

Shows your browser checks in the ScamShield desktop app, next to the checks you make there. Results are passed to the app on this computer through Chrome's native messaging: only the channel, score, risk level, pattern and warning-sign names, never the message text.

First-time setup
  1. Build the desktop app (mvn package) so target\scamshield-1.1.0.jar exists.
  2. In the project folder, run powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts\install-native-host.ps1 once. It registers the connector for Chrome and Edge for your Windows account only.
  3. Switch on Send results to the desktop dashboard above and press Test connection.
  4. Open the desktop app (ScamShield.bat). Its Dashboard updates by itself as you check messages here.

Trusted senders and websites

One entry per line. A trusted entry skips only the checks about who sent the message or where a link points. The message text is always checked, because trusted accounts can be hacked or imitated.

Rules

Turn rules off or change how many points they add (0–100). A total of 25 or more is Medium risk, 50 High and 75 Critical.

About & privacy

ScamShield checks messages with a fixed set of explainable rules. It does not use AI, does not look links up online and cannot verify who really sent a message. Its result is a warning, not proof.

  • No network access: the extension's security policy blocks all outgoing connections.
  • Screenshots and QR codes are read on this device by bundled copies of jsQR and Tesseract.js (Apache-2.0).
  • Inline warnings can read only the sites you switch on here, and you can turn them off at any time.
  • Lost money or shared details? Call 1930 or report at cybercrime.gov.in.
